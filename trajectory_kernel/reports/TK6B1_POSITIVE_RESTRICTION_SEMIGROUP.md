# TK6B.1 — The Finite Positive Restriction Semigroup

**Status:** exact cone and Hilbert-basis audit complete in the baseline-free
level-11 closure

**Provenance:** baseline-free

**Last updated:** 25 September 2026

## Result

Let $A$ be the matrix whose columns are the multiplicity-free
$D_5\downarrow B_4$ interlacing packets in the finite closure generated by the
cumulative HFT spectrum through level 11. Outer-conjugate $D_5$ parents have
identical restrictions and are represented by one unoriented column.

The resulting matrix has

$$
A\in\mathbb Z^{73\times84},
$$

with rank 73 and kernel rank 11. Its columns generate the rational cone

$$
C=A\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{84}
$$

and the integer packet semigroup

$$
S=A\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{84}.
$$

The exact finite result is

$$
S=C\cap\mathbb Z^{73}.
$$

Thus this particular finite semigroup is saturated: an integral target in the
level-11 row lattice has a positive integral $D_5$ lift if and only if it lies
in the rational packet cone. Equivalently, the complete finite liftability
test consists of the cone's facet inequalities; there is no additional
integrality obstruction.

This is not yet an all-orders theorem. The closure, its 73 rows and its 2,508
facets were derived from HFT data only through level 11.

## 1. Packet domain and lattice

For every $B_4$ top label

$$
\mu=[\ell,m,r,s],
$$

the unoriented parent family is

$$
[\ell,m,r,k,s-k]_{D_5},
\qquad 0\leq k\leq\lfloor s/2\rfloor.
$$

Choosing $a_4\leq a_5$ gives exactly one representative from each
outer-chirality orbit. The code verifies that all 84 resulting columns are
distinct, nonzero and binary. Binary entries express the multiplicity-free
$D_5\downarrow B_4$ branching rule.

The 73 all-chiral columns, one for each top weight, form a square triangular
submatrix of determinant one. Therefore the group generated by the packet
columns is the full ambient lattice $\mathbb Z^{73}$. The saturation statement
below is consequently relative to the ordinary integral $B_4$ multiplicity
lattice, not a proper sublattice.

## 2. Total unimodularity fails

The matrix is not totally unimodular. An exact certificate uses the three
$B_4$ rows

$$
[5,1,0,0],\quad[3,2,0,0],\quad[4,0,1,0]
$$

and the three $D_5$ packet columns

$$
[3,1,1,0,0],\quad[5,0,1,0,0],\quad[4,2,0,0,0].
$$

The resulting minor is

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&1\\
1&0&1\\
1&1&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$

whose determinant is 2. Hence total unimodularity cannot supply the desired
integrality theorem.

This obstruction does not imply that the semigroup has holes. Normality is a
weaker property than total unimodularity, and in this closure it survives.

## 3. Exact cone

The exact PPL double-description calculation gives:

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Ambient dimension | 73 |
| Cone dimension | 73 |
| Packet columns | 84 |
| Extreme rays | 84 |
| Irredundant facets | 2,508 |
| Linear equations | 0 |

Every packet column is therefore an extreme ray; none is rationally redundant.
Facet support sizes range from 1 to 42. Of the 2,508 primitive facet
functionals, 2,289 have maximum coefficient magnitude 1 and 219 require a
coefficient of magnitude 2. This already shows that the complete inequality
system is richer than coordinatewise positivity or a single elementary Hall
condition.

The implementation retains every exact facet functional and evaluates targets
against the full list. The report does not print all 2,508 inequalities because
TK6B.2 will first classify them under shifts and poset symmetries.

## 4. Hilbert basis and saturation

PyNormaliz 2.24 computes the Hilbert basis of

$$
C\cap\mathbb Z^{73}.
$$

It has exactly 84 elements, and its set of vectors is exactly the set of 84
original packet columns. There are no additional Hilbert generators. Normaliz
also returns that the original monoid is integrally closed.

Because the packet columns generate the full ambient lattice, these checks give

$$
A\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{84}
=
A\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{84}\cap\mathbb Z^{73}.
$$

This calculation was checked against a synthetic determinant-two semigroup
with a genuine Hilbert hole. The same code correctly finds the extra generator
$(1,1,1)$ and declares that synthetic monoid nonnormal. Thus the level-11
normality result is not a consequence of treating cone generators as a Hilbert
basis by definition.

## 6. Interpretation and limits

TK6B.1 separates the two possible obstructions cleanly:

1. **Rational obstruction:** a target violates a facet inequality.
2. **Integral obstruction:** a target lies in the cone but outside the packet
   semigroup.

The second obstruction is absent in the complete level-11 closure. Therefore
the next analytic task can focus on the rational inequalities. It is not
necessary, in this finite window, to solve a separate congruence or hole
problem after establishing all facet inequalities.

The following statements remain open:

- saturation for arbitrary finite downward-closed interlacing domains;
- stabilization of the 2,508 facets into finitely many shift families;
- preservation of all such families by the HFT/GMSZ recurrences;
- an all-orders proof that every cumulative HFT target satisfies them.

No chirality orientation is selected: outer-conjugate parents were collapsed
only because their $B_4$ packet columns are identical. Once an unoriented
packet decomposition exists, each packet may be assigned either outer
orientation without changing its restriction.

## Reproduction

Install the exact optional backend listed in `requirements-tk6b.txt`, then run

```text
./bin/sage-run -m trajectory_kernel.restriction_semigroup --self-test
./bin/sage-run -m trajectory_kernel.restriction_semigroup --max-level 11
./bin/sage-run -m trajectory_kernel.restriction_semigroup --max-level 11 --verbose
```

If PyNormaliz is installed outside the active Sage environment, pass its
directory with `--normaliz-path`. The PPL cone, determinant certificate and
integral HFT witnesses can be run without Normaliz using `--skip-normaliz`, but
that option deliberately omits the Hilbert-basis and saturation theorem.
