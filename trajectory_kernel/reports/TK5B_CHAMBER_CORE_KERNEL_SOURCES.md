# TK5b — Transported Chambers, Residual Core and Kernel Sources

**Status:** complete through HFT level 11  
**Last updated:** 24 September 2026  
**Provenance:** baseline-free

## 1. Question

TK5a verified that the empirical support and multiplicity-shift chambers of
Georgoudis--Minahan--Ström--Zoumis (GMSZ) agree exactly with the HFT-derived
$SO(9)$ spectrum through level 11. TK5b asks whether this chamber structure
organizes the restriction-kernel corrections required for positive cumulative
$SO(10)$ lifts.

There are two distinct questions:

1. Are the finite $D_5\downarrow B_4$ kernel directions generated by weights
   that are not themselves transported from lower levels?
2. Can the level-dependent coefficients of those directions be chosen using
   sources only where the corresponding $SO(9)$ top weight lies in the GMSZ
   residual core?

The implementation is `trajectory_kernel/chamber_core.py`. It derives the HFT
spectrum, branching closure, kernel basis and positivity constraints without
importing the protected $SO(10)$ completion.

## 2. Deterministic chamber decomposition

GMSZ state that their shift types are considered in the order $\ell$, $m$,
$r$, and that the largest admissible positive step $k$ is used in the first
applicable family. TK5b adopts this as an explicit selection convention.

Every nonzero irrep in $r_n^9$ for $n>6$ is therefore assigned exactly one of
four provenances:

- $\ell$-transport from level $n-k$;
- $m$-transport from level $n-3k$;
- $r$-transport from level $n-6k$;
- residual core, if none of the three relations applies.

The resulting decomposition is:

| Level | $\ell$-transported | $m$-transported | $r$-transported | Core | Total irreps |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 7 | 9 | 1 | 0 | 13 | 23 |
| 8 | 13 | 2 | 0 | 17 | 32 |
| 9 | 18 | 3 | 0 | 22 | 43 |
| 10 | 24 | 2 | 0 | 30 | 56 |
| 11 | 31 | 4 | 0 | 37 | 72 |

Counting multiplicities rather than distinct irreps gives:

| Level | $\ell$ mass | $m$ mass | $r$ mass | Core mass |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 7 | 10 | 1 | 0 | 28 |
| 8 | 16 | 2 | 0 | 61 |
| 9 | 26 | 4 | 0 | 133 |
| 10 | 45 | 3 | 0 | 280 |
| 11 | 75 | 6 | 0 | 576 |

Every selected transport equality holds exactly. No $r$-transport is
admissible in this finite window.

The core is not a thin set: both its number of irreps and its total
multiplicity grow across the tested levels. TK5b therefore does not establish
that the full $SO(9)$ spectrum is determined by finitely many boundary values.

## 3. Kernel-generator lineage

The level-11 branching closure has 11 natural integral kernel generators. For
a GMSZ shift family $f\in\{\ell,m,r\}$, let $\sigma_f$ shift the corresponding
first, second or third $D_5$ Dynkin coordinate. The exact corrected shift is

$$
T_f^k(K)=\sigma_f^kK
-s_{\mathrm{ch}}\!\left(\operatorname{Res}(\sigma_f^kK)\right),
$$

where $s_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is the fixed all-chiral integral section. By
construction, $T_f^k(K)$ again restricts to zero.

For every kernel generator whose first HFT appearance is GMSZ-transported,
TK5b checks that applying the corresponding corrected shift to the source
generator gives the target generator exactly.

The first-appearance classification is:

| Kernel generators | First-appearance provenance |
| --- | --- |
| $A_0$ | pre-chamber, level 6 |
| $A_1,\ldots,A_5$ | $\ell$-transported |
| $B_0$ | $m$-transported from $A_0$ |
| $B_1,B_2$ | $\ell$-transported |
| $C_0$ | residual core, level 10 |
| $C_1$ | $\ell$-transported |

Thus the family-$B$ object previously called a seed is not independent once
the GMSZ $m$ direction is admitted: its kernel relation is the exact corrected
$m$-shift of $A_0$.

All 11 finite kernel generators are generated from only two nontransported
objects:

$$
A_0\quad\text{and}\quad C_0.
$$

Here $A_0$ predates the claimed GMSZ domain, while $C_0$ is a genuine residual
core object. This is an exact statement about the level-11 finite branching
lattice. It does not prove that new nontransported kernel seeds never appear
at higher levels.

## 4. Core-supported coordinate sources

Let $z_{F,\ell}(n)$ be a kernel coordinate in a positive cumulative lift and
define its corrected-shift source by

$$
s_{F,0}(n)=z_{F,0}(n),
$$

$$
s_{F,\ell}(n)=z_{F,\ell}(n)-z_{F,\ell-1}(n-1)
\qquad(\ell>0).
$$

TK4b showed that allowing sources only at $\ell=0$ is feasible through level
8 and fails at level 9. TK5b tests the more structured rule:

> A source at $(F,\ell,n)$ is allowed only when the generator's $SO(9)$ top
> weight is actually present in $r_n^9$ and is classified as residual core
> (or lies in the explicitly marked pre-chamber range).

Exact integer feasibility gives:

| Source policy | Feasible through | First obstruction |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Strict $\ell=0$ boundary only | 8 | 9 |
| GMSZ residual core | 11 | none in window |

One solver witness for the core-supported rule has the following nonzero
sources:

| Level | Sources |
| ---: | --- |
| 7 | $A_0:+1$ |
| 8 | $A_1:+1$ |
| 9 | $A_1:+2$ |
| 10 | $A_2:+3$, $B_0:+1$, $A_1:+3$ |
| 11 | $B_1:-1$, $A_2:+5$, $B_0:+2$, $A_1:+1$, $A_0:+2$ |

The witness has 11 nonzero source terms and absolute source charge 22. These
numbers were not minimized and have no canonical significance. The important
facts are that every displayed source lies at a GMSZ-core position and every
resulting cumulative $SO(10)$ character is positive and restricts exactly to
the intended cumulative HFT $SO(9)$ character.

The negative $B_1$ source at level 11 is not a negative representation
multiplicity. It is a decrease relative to corrected-shift transport; the
resulting kernel coordinates and all final $SO(10)$ coefficients remain
nonnegative.

## 5. Interpretation and limits

TK5b gives a coherent explanation of the TK4b level-9 failure:

- strict boundary generation was too restrictive because a fixed positive
  $\ell$ weight eventually leaves the transported upper chamber and enters
  the residual core;
- allowing a new kernel-coordinate source precisely at that transition
  restores positive lifts through level 11;
- the finite kernel directions themselves form corrected-shift descendants of
  the two nontransported objects $A_0$ and $C_0$.

This is encouraging evidence that the $SO(10)$ ambiguity is organized by the
same chamber boundary as the $SO(9)$ multiplicities. It is not yet an analytic
selection principle:

- the GMSZ shifts are empirical;
- the core-supported path need not be unique;
- the displayed witness was selected only by feasibility;
- the core grows with level;
- higher levels may introduce new nontransported kernel seeds.

The next useful question is whether the core-source feasibility problem has a
closed recurrence or finite generating set, and whether the additional
oscillator grading noted in hep-th/0310292 selects a distinguished path among
the feasible ones.
